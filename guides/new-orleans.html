Quick destination guide

New Orleans, LA trip planning guide

A walkable, food- and music-driven city trip. Most first visits center on the French Quarter, with day trips out to the Garden District and Warehouse District museums.

This is a fast-build planning guide — the essentials to get moving, not the full depth of our flagship destination hubs. Always confirm current prices, hours, and conditions before booking.

Photo: Justin Watt via Wikimedia Commons (Public domain)

Best time to visit

October through early April is the sweet spot — mild temperatures, lower humidity, and thinner crowds outside of festival weekends. Spring (especially around Mardi Gras and Jazz Fest) is the most crowded and highest-priced stretch. Hurricane season runs June–November, with August and September the most volatile — build flexibility into trips booked in that window.

What to budget

Mid-range travelers commonly spend $120–$270 per person per day (3-star hotel, sit-down meals, a few paid activities). Budget travel using hostels, casual food, and the streetcar can run closer to $50–$120 per day. Hotel rooms alone commonly run $120–$250 per night depending on season and neighborhood.

Getting around

The French Quarter is compact and walkable — most sights are within a 15–20 minute walk of each other, so a car is optional if you're staying central. Streetcars connect the Quarter to the Garden District, Uptown, and City Park for $1.25 a ride, or a Jazzy Pass ($3/day, $8/3-day, $15/7-day) for unlimited rides. Rideshare and taxis fill the gaps for anything outside walking or streetcar range.

Where to stay

French Quarter

The most walkable base and the center of the action — music, food, and history within a compact grid. Expect noise and higher rates near Bourbon Street; quieter blocks are a short walk away.

Photo: Diego Delso via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)

Garden District / Uptown

Quieter, tree-lined, and historic, with easy streetcar access back into the Quarter. A good fit if you want distance from late-night noise without losing convenience.

Photo: Infrogmation of New Orleans via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Warehouse District

New Orleans' arts and museum hub, home to the National WWII Museum and a strong restaurant scene, a short walk or streetcar ride from the Quarter.

Photo: Infrogmation of New Orleans via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Faubourg Marigny

A quieter, more local alternative just past the Quarter, known for boutique guesthouses and a strong live-music strip on Frenchmen Street.

Photo: Infrogmation of New Orleans via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

Worth doing

Jackson Square & Café du Monde

The classic first stop — beignets, street performers, and the square itself. Go early to beat both heat and crowds.

Photo: justinsomnia.org via Wikimedia Commons (Public domain)

St. Charles Avenue streetcar ride

A cheap, scenic way to see the Garden District's historic mansions without booking a tour.

Photo: Infrogmation of New Orleans via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

National WWII Museum

A large, well-regarded museum in the Warehouse District — plan a half day, not a quick stop.

Photo: Infrogmation of New Orleans via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0)

Frenchmen Street live music

The local alternative to Bourbon Street for hearing real jazz and brass bands, in the Marigny.

Photo: Infrogmation of New Orleans via Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

French Quarter food or ghost tour

A guided tour is worth it for context on the city's history; book ahead for popular evening slots.

Quick answers

What's the best time to visit New Orleans?

October through early April for the best mix of weather, price, and crowd levels — avoid peak festival weekends if you want lower rates and easier reservations.

Do I need a car in New Orleans?

Not if you're staying in or near the French Quarter. Walking, the streetcar, and rideshare cover almost everything most visitors need.

What should I budget per day?

Plan on roughly $120–$270 per person per day for a mid-range trip, or $50–$120 for a tighter budget using hostels and casual food.

Where should I stay?

The French Quarter for walkability and nightlife, the Garden District or Uptown for a quieter base, or the Warehouse District if museums and restaurants are the priority.

Is hurricane season a real risk for my trip?

Hurricane season runs June–November, with August and September the most active. It doesn't rule out a trip, but build in travel-insurance and flexible-booking options if traveling in that window.
